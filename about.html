A commercial insurer that still underwrites by hand

We're not the biggest carrier in the market, and we've stopped trying to be. Meridian stays small enough that a person not an algorithm reads your file before your rate is set.

1912

Founded in Chicago, insuring a single machine shop on the South Side

4 decisions that shaped the company

1912

A single-line start

Meridian opened writing one product — fire coverage for machine shops — because the founders refused to underwrite a trade they hadn't walked the floor of.

Kept claims in-house

While competitors began outsourcing claims to third-party administrators, Meridian built its own claims desk and never closed it — a decision that still shapes hiring today.

1968
1997

Named agents, not call queues

Every policyholder was assigned one agent for the life of their account, replacing the rotating-representative model most of the industry still uses.

Cyber added as a core line

Meridian began underwriting cyber liability in-house rather than reselling a third-party facility, after seeing how often claims fell into coverage gaps between policies.

2019

Four rules our underwriters don't bend

These aren't values on a wall poster — they're written into how policies actually get priced and approved.

Plain language, always

If a clause needs a lawyer to explain it to you, we rewrite the clause — not just the summary.

No surprise exclusions

Every exclusion is disclosed at quote stage. None are discovered for the first time during a claim.

Underwriters visit the trade

Before we write a new industry class, someone from Meridian spends a day on-site to understand the actual risk.

Pay first, dispute later

Where a claim is ambiguous, we pay the undisputed portion immediately rather than holding the full amount hostage to review.
